⚡ DOWNSTRIKE

← Back to the map

Downstrike — the weather word for a lightning bolt that hits the ground. A live lightning map centered on your home. No accounts, no sign-ups: everything runs in your browser, using free public data.

01 · How the lightning is tracked

Strikes come from Blitzortung.org, a worldwide network of radio receivers run by volunteers. Nobody points a camera at the sky — the network hears every bolt and finds it with a stopwatch:

  • A lightning bolt is a radio transmitter. Its huge burst of electric current sends out a radio pulse that travels thousands of kilometres at the speed of light. (It's the crackle you hear on AM radio during a storm.)
  • Volunteers listen at home. Each station is a small antenna kit with a GPS clock accurate to a millionth of a second, which records the exact moment the pulse arrives.
  • Timing finds the bolt. Radio travels about 300 metres every microsecond, so stations farther away hear the pulse a little later. Compare arrival times at four or more stations and the possible locations cross at one point: the strike. It's GPS in reverse — many receivers timing one flash to find it.
  • Usually 20–40 stations hear each strike, pinning it to about a kilometre. It reaches your screen 5–15 seconds after the real flash.

Downstrike keeps a live connection to this feed and receives every located strike on Earth — often 50+ per second. Because it's a volunteer network, a strike is occasionally missed or placed a kilometre or two off. The data is free for personal use.

02 · What's on the map

  • Strike dots. A new strike lands with a white flash, then cools like an ember — the color tells you its age:
    now
    2 min
    8 min
    16 min
    30 min, then gone
    So a storm paints its own trail: white at the front, embers behind.
  • Thunder rings. Each strike sends out a ring growing at 343 m/s — the real speed of sound. When a ring crosses your house, that's the exact moment you'd hear the rumble.
  • Home rings. Dashed circles at 10, 25 and 50 km show distance at a glance. The amber glow on the outer ring points toward recent strikes and brightens as a storm gets closer.
  • WIND. Hundreds of particles drift with the real wind (live data, refreshed every 30 minutes).
  • RADAR. Live rain radar, refreshed every 5 minutes, off by default. (The free tiles get blurry if you zoom way in.)

03 · The thunder sound

The thunder is generated live by code — no recordings — and shaped by the strike's real distance, the same way air shapes real thunder:

LayerWhat it isWhen you hear it
BoomA deep, blunt impact with an echo Only within ~12 km — harder the closer the strike
Sub-bass thumpA low 50 Hz punch you feel Within ~25 km
RumbleA rolling growl with random peaks Always — it's all that's left of a far-away strike

The farther the strike, the quieter it gets (about 10× softer by 50 km), the deeper it sounds (air soaks up the high notes first), and the longer and slower it rolls. Flip SOUND on to hear a preview — browsers need one click on the page before they'll play audio.

04 · The controls

  • ALERT slider. Pick a distance (5–50 km). Any strike inside it fires the red banner and a thunder clap at that strike's real distance.
  • Readouts. Closest strike of the last hour (with direction), strikes per minute within 100 km, and live wind at home.
  • ⚡ STORM. Flies you to the most active storm on Earth right now — press again to hop to the next one. Away from home, you hear what you'd hear standing at the crosshair in the middle of the screen: any strike inside its dashed 50 km ring plays at its real distance.
  • ⌂. Brings you back home. Press it while at home to change your address.
  • ALERTS (iOS app). Turn it on and your phone gets a push notification when the National Weather Service issues a severe thunderstorm or tornado warning for where you are — even with the app closed. These are the official warnings, checked every few minutes.
  • Next hour rain (iOS app). A minute-by-minute strip showing when rain will start and stop at your location over the coming hour, powered by Weather.

05 · Privacy & sources

Your address is looked up once and saved only in your browser. No accounts. Visits are counted with GoatCounter, a privacy-friendly counter that uses no cookies and collects no personal data.

One exception, and only if you turn on ALERTS in the iOS app: your device's push token and your location rounded to about a kilometre are stored on our server, because something has to check the warnings while your phone is asleep. Nothing else is stored, and turning ALERTS off stops the checks.
