⚡ DOWNSTRIKE

← Back to the map

Downstrike — the meteorologist's term for a cloud-to-ground lightning bolt. A real-time, hyper-local storm watcher centered on your home. No accounts, no API keys, no server: everything runs in your browser, fed by free public data.

01 · The lightning feed

Strikes come from Blitzortung.org, a community network of thousands of volunteer-operated VLF radio receivers around the world. A lightning discharge emits a radio pulse (a "sferic"); when several stations hear the same pulse, the tiny differences in arrival time pinpoint the strike — the same time-of-arrival principle GPS uses in reverse.

Downstrike holds a WebSocket connection to the Blitzortung feed and receives every located strike on Earth, typically 30–100 per second during active weather, each as an LZW-compressed JSON message. Latency from the actual flash is usually 5–15 seconds. If the connection drops, the app rotates through the feed's mirror servers automatically — the status dot under the logo shows the live state.

Accuracy caveat: it's a volunteer network. Detection in populated regions is very good, but an occasional strike is missed or placed a kilometre or two off. The data is free for personal, non-commercial use.

02 · The map & visual mechanics

The basemap is CARTO's dark-matter style over OpenStreetMap data, rendered by MapLibre GL. Everything else is drawn on canvas layers on top:

  • Strike dots. A new strike lands with a bright white flash, then cools as it ages — the color is the clock:
    now
    2 min
    8 min
    16 min
    30 min, then gone
    A storm therefore paints its own track: fresh white at the leading edge, embers trailing behind.
  • Thunder rings. Each strike near the view emits a ring expanding at 343 m/s — the real speed of sound. When a ring crosses your house, that's the moment you hear the rumble. Rings fade out at 50 km, past which thunder is rarely audible.
  • Home rings. Dashed circles at 10, 25 and 50 km around your home make distance readable at a glance.
  • Approach glow. The amber arc on the 50 km ring points toward recent activity. It's computed from the last 10 minutes of strikes within 120 km, each weighted by 1 / (5 + distance) — close strikes dominate. The arc narrows as the activity concentrates in one direction and brightens as it gets closer.
  • Wind flow (WIND). Hundreds of particles drift across the map along the real wind field: a 5×5 grid of current wind from Open-Meteo (~±130 km around home), bilinearly interpolated, refreshed every 30 minutes. Trails fade like long-exposure photography. It's deliberately subtle — lightning is the star.
  • Rain radar (RADAR). The latest RainViewer composite radar frame, refreshed every 5 minutes, off by default. RainViewer's free tiles stop at zoom level 7, so the radar goes soft when you zoom way in.

03 · The thunder sound

Alert sounds are synthesized live with WebAudio — no recordings — and are shaped by the actual distance of the strike, mimicking how air filters sound over kilometres:

LayerWhat it isWhen you hear it
BoomBlunt low-mid impact with a ragged echo 90 ms later Only within ~12 km, harder and brighter the closer the strike
Sub-bass thumpA 50→30 Hz sine drop — the chest-punch Within ~25 km
RumbleBrown noise rolling through 3–6 random peaks Always — and it's all that's left of a distant strike

Distance changes everything at once: loudness falls roughly tenfold from overhead to 50 km; a low-pass filter sweeps from ~465 Hz down to ~85 Hz (far thunder is pure bass); duration stretches from ~2 s to ~6 s; and far thunder swells in slowly instead of starting with a bang. Toggling SOUND on plays a preview clap at a simulated 6 km — browsers require one click on the page before any audio is allowed.

04 · Features

  • Alert slider. Sets the alarm radius (5–50 km). Any strike inside it triggers the red banner (distance + compass bearing) and a thunder clap at the strike's real distance, throttled to one clap per 8 seconds.
  • Closest strike. Distance and bearing of the nearest strike over the last 60 minutes.
  • Strikes / min. Live intensity meter counting strikes within 100 km — watch it climb as a cell peaks.
  • Wind at home. Current speed, gusts and direction from Open-Meteo, refreshed every 10 minutes.
  • ⚡ STORM. No storm nearby? This scans the last 10 minutes of the global feed, grids it into 1° cells, and flies you to the most active storm on the planet — at the same zoom as the home view, so strikes, thunder rings, radar and wind all work exactly as they do at home. Press it again to hop to the next-busiest storm; it always skips the one already on screen. While you're away from home, you hear what you'd hear standing at the map center — marked by a faint pulsing crosshair inside a dashed "thunder audible · 50 km" ring. Any strike within that ring plays thunder at its real distance (booms up close, faint rumbles from far cells), and a thunder ring sweeping across the crosshair marks the moment its sound arrives. Home alerts still fire in the background.
  • ⌂. When you've flown away, one press returns you home. When you're already home, it opens the address settings.

05 · Privacy & sources

Your address is geocoded once via OpenStreetMap Nominatim when you set it, then stored only in this browser's localStorage. Nothing about you is sent anywhere else — no accounts, no analytics, no backend.
